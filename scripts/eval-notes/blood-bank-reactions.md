## ABO discrepancies

Forward typing = patient RBC + anti-A, anti-B. Reverse = patient serum + A1 cells, B cells. They should agree.

Group I (weak or missing reverse): newborns (no antibodies until 3-6 mos), elderly, hypogammaglobulinemia, BMT. Fix: incubate at RT 15-30 min, if still weak 4C for 15 min with auto control and O cells.

Group II (weak or missing forward): ABO subgroups, leukemia, acquired B (colon or gastric ca, bacterial deacetylase turns A antigen's N-acetylgalactosamine into galactosamine so group A looks AB), excess soluble Ag in ovarian cyst fluid. Acquired B: patient's own anti-B does not react with own cells.

Group III (extra rxns from plasma proteins): rouleaux from multiple myeloma, Waldenstrom, dextran, Wharton's jelly in cord samples. Fix: saline replacement, or wash cells.

Group IV (misc): cold autoantibodies, polyagglutination, unexpected alloantibody e.g. A2 person with anti-A1. Fix for A2: Dolichos biflorus lectin agglutinates A1 not A2.

## Transfusion reactions

AHTR: usually clerical error, ABO incompatible. Within 24h. Fever, chills, flank pain, hemoglobinuria, DIC, renal failure. IgM activates complement to C9, intravascular.
Workup when ANY reaction suspected: stop transfusion, keep IV line open w/ NSS, clerical check at bedside, send post sample. Lab: visual check for hemolysis, DAT on post sample, recheck ABO.

DHTR: 3-14 days after, anamnestic IgG. Falling hgb, jaundice, new positive DAT, extravascular. Kidd abs are the classic (anti-Jka), dosage effect and drop below detectable, so pre-tx screen can be negative.

FNHTR: temp rise of 1C or more. Cytokines in stored product or pt anti-leukocyte abs. Diagnosis of exclusion. Prevention = leukoreduction.

Allergic: hives, itching. IgE vs donor plasma proteins. Only reaction where you may restart the same unit after antihistamine and symptoms resolve.

Anaphylactic: no fever, shock in minutes, IgA deficient pt with anti-IgA. Give washed RBCs or IgA deficient donor products.

TRALI: within 6 h, hypoxemia, bilateral infiltrates, NO circulatory overload. Donor anti-HLA or anti-HNA vs pt WBCs, so mostly plasma-rich products from multiparous women. BNP normal, BP often low.
TACO: within 6-12 h, HTN, dyspnea, elevated BNP, responds to diuretics. Slow rate is prevention.

Septic: platelets most common because of 20-24C storage, gram positive (Staph epidermidis). RBC units at 1-6C: Yersinia enterocolitica, Pseudomonas. Fever + hypotension, gram stain & culture both unit and pt.

TA-GVHD: viable donor T lymphocytes engraft in immunocompromised pt or HLA-haploidentical relative donor. Almost always fatal. Pancytopenia is the giveaway. Prevention: irradiation 25 Gy minimum. Leukoreduction does not prevent it.

## Storage

Whole blood and pRBC 1-6C. CPD, CPD2 = 21 days. CPDA-1 = 35 days. AS-1, AS-3, AS-5 = 42 days.
Open system pRBC (washed) = 24 h at 1-6C.
Platelets = 20-24C with continuous agitation, 5 days.
FFP = -18C or colder, 1 year. Thawed at 30-37C, use within 24 h stored 1-6C.
Cryo = -18C, 1 year. Pooled and thawed, 4 h at 20-24C. Has fibrinogen, FVIII, FXIII, vWF.

Storage lesion: ATP down, 2,3-DPG down (shift left), pH down, K up, plasma hgb up.
